## Full detail

### 1. [HIGH] Money going OUT has no date, so every closed month silently rewrites itself

- Kind: temporal-drift | Manual workaround adequate: false

**Scenario.** 8 July: Amy's S$120 PayNow lands, Michelle taps Mark paid, paid_at = 8 July. 2 August she closes July: Insights > Last month reads S$4,200 and her bank statement for July reads S$4,200 in. She ticks it off. 6 August: one tin on a different July order (S$96, paid 22 July) arrived squashed, so she PayNows S$18 back and taps Record a refund. 12 August: Bea cancels her 26 July order (S$95, paid by PayNow 24 July); Michelle taps Cancel order, the alert tells her to send S$95 back, and she does. 20 August someone asks for July again. Insights > Last month now reads S$4,087. The July bank statement still reads S$4,200. August's Insights shows nothing at all for the S$113 that physically left her account in August. July's figure will keep moving every time anyone refunds or cancels an old order, so no month can ever be closed.

**Why a defect audit would miss it.** Migration 0036 fixed exactly half of this and the fix looked complete. paid_at was added specifically so revenue lands on the day the money arrived rather than the day the order was placed, and order_refunds was added specifically so partial and Stripe-side refunds stop being invisible. Both were verified as applied. But paid_at dated the inflow only. order_refunds.created_at is written on every row and is genuinely there in the live schema, yet fetchAdminOrders never loads it (admin-db.ts:119 selects `order_refunds(amount_cents)` and nothing else), AdminOrder has no field for it (order.ts:212-231), and both reporting screens fold the refund total into the ORDER's bucket rather than the refund's own. A defect audit asks whether the subtraction is arithmetically right, and it is. Nobody asked which day the subtraction belongs to, because that question had just been answered for the other direction.

**Consequence.** There is no period over which the app's revenue can be made to equal the bank's net movement, which was the whole purpose of adding paid_at. Worse, cancels are not merely mis-dated, they are erased: cancelAndRefundOrder (admin-db.ts:610) writes no order_refunds row at any point, and sets status = 'cancelled', which drops the order out of the revenue set entirely (analytics:52 and dashboard page.tsx:71 both filter `status !== 'cancelled'`). So a paid order cancelled in a later month removes its full total from an already-reconciled earlier month and records the outflow nowhere. There is also no refunds list anywhere in the panel: to find every cent that went back in August she would have to open every order individually, and cancels would not appear even then.

**Suggested resolution.** Two changes. (1) Load order_refunds.created_at into AdminOrder and bucket each refund as a negative on its OWN day rather than subtracting it from the order's paid day, so keptCents stops being an order-level idea and becomes a dated cash-out event. (2) Make cancelAndRefundOrder write an order_refunds row for whatever actually went back (the Stripe refund amount, or the manualRefundDue amount she is told to send by hand), and keep a cancelled-but-paid order in the revenue set carrying that offsetting refund, instead of deleting it from history. That way the sum of order_refunds over a month is the outflow side of the bank statement, and a month once closed stays closed. A small refunds-this-month figure on Insights falls out of it for free.

**Evidence.** src/app/admin/(panel)/analytics/page.tsx:60 `earnedOn = (o) => dayOf(o.paidAt ?? o.createdAt)` and :63 `keptCents = (o) => o.totalCents - (o.refundedCents ?? 0)`, applied together at :87 and in the range fold; src/app/admin/(panel)/page.tsx:70-72 does the same subtraction with no date at all. src/lib/admin-db.ts:119 selects only `order_refunds(amount_cents)`; src/lib/order.ts:212-231 AdminOrder has refundedCents but no refund dates. src/lib/admin-db.ts:610-728 cancelAndRefundOrder contains no insert into order_refunds; it sets status='cancelled' at :690-697. supabase/migrations/0036_payment_dates_refunds_and_optouts.sql creates order_refunds.created_at, confirmed present on the live database (project ddwesutmtlytbcluqcuc). fetchOrderRefunds (admin-db.ts:786) is the only reader of created_at and feeds a per-order list, not reporting.

### 2. [HIGH] A deposit is real money in the bank that now appears in no money figure at all, and carries no date

- Kind: reconciliation-hole | Manual workaround adequate: false

**Scenario.** 14 June: a customer books a S$400 wedding cake for 20 September and PayNows S$150 to hold the date. Michelle types 150.00 into Deposit and taps Record. payment_status stays pending, which is correct, since a deposit must not unlock baking. Her June bank statement shows +S$150. She opens Insights that week: Lifetime revenue and the range revenue count only paid orders, so the S$150 is not in either; To collect reads S$250 for that order rather than S$400, because it subtracts the deposit; Booked next 14 days is a September figure. The S$150 exists on exactly one screen in the whole app, inside that one order's modal, as a number with no date on it. On 18 September the balance lands, she taps Mark balance paid, and the full S$400 books to 18 September. June under-reads by S$150 against the June bank and September over-reads by S$150 against the September bank, and nothing in the database can say otherwise. The same hole swallows a payment that partially settles: a customer means to send S$200 and sends S$180, and since full-payment-only is the rule, the only way to record the S$180 is as a 'deposit' that is dated nowhere and booked nowhere.

**Why a defect audit would miss it.** This one is a fix that left a hole, and I can point at where. The 07-30 audit raised it as gap #9 and the challenge note REFUTED the claim 'the deposit is in neither To collect nor Paid revenue' on the specific grounds that toCollectCents summed the full total_cents, so the money did at least show up somewhere. The applied fix changed toCollectCents to `Math.max(0, o.totalCents - (o.depositCents ?? 0))`, which corrects the overstatement of what is owed and, in doing so, makes the refuted claim true for the first time. The rest of that gap's energy went into the cancel path, which was genuinely fixed with deposit_outstanding_cents, so the deposit feature reads as reviewed and settled. Nobody re-checked the ordinary, non-cancel case afterwards, and the settled decision that a deposit does not unlock baking made 'a deposit is not payment' feel like a complete answer, when the money-received question is separate from the may-she-bake question.

**Consequence.** Deposits are the one part of the business where money and food are furthest apart in time, which is exactly when dating matters most, and they are the only mechanism the model has for a payment that partially settled. Every deposit is a hole in two months at once: the month it arrived reads low, the month the balance arrived reads high, and there is no total-deposits-held figure anywhere to net against. deposit_cents is also a single mutable integer with no history: recordDeposit overwrites it and 0 clears it to null, so a corrected typo leaves no trace. The live schema confirms there is no deposit_paid_at and no deposit_returned_at. One further symptom: a deposit kept as a cancellation fee, which is the reason a home bakery takes deposits at all, has no representation either, since clearDepositOwed states outright that it 'only clears the reminder, it does not move any money', so money she legitimately keeps is revenue the books never see.

**Suggested resolution.** Stop treating a deposit as a scratch number on the order and treat it as a dated receipt, without touching the settled paid-before-baking rule. The cheapest shape that reuses what already exists is to record money received the same way money returned is recorded: an order_payments row (amount, date, via manual/stripe) written whenever a deposit is recorded and whenever the balance is marked paid, with paid_at kept as the moment the order became fully settled. Revenue then buckets on payment rows rather than on the order, so June gets S$150 and September gets S$250, and a partial settlement becomes an honest S$180 receipt rather than a mislabelled deposit. Short of that, the minimum is a deposit_paid_at column plus a Deposits held tile on Insights, so at least the money is visible and dated somewhere, and a third answer on the cancel prompt for a deposit kept as a fee.

**Evidence.** src/app/admin/(panel)/analytics/page.tsx:77-79 `toCollectCents` subtracts depositCents; :52 and :87 restrict every revenue figure to `paymentStatus === 'paid'`; src/app/admin/(panel)/page.tsx:70-72 likewise. src/lib/admin-db.ts:341-360 recordDeposit writes only deposit_cents and caps at total. src/lib/admin-db.ts:732-739 clearDepositOwed clears the reminder only, and the panel says so at orders/page.tsx:1163-1168. Grep confirms depositCents is referenced in exactly two places outside that one modal: analytics:78 and customer-contact.ts:46. Live schema query on project ddwesutmtlytbcluqcuc returns deposit_cents and deposit_outstanding_cents on orders and no deposit date column of any kind. docs/logic-gap-audit-2026-07-30.md gap #9 challenge note, point 2, is the refutation that the To collect fix has now invalidated.

### 3. [MEDIUM] The admin panel never shows the discount, so Michelle cannot explain a total that is smaller than its own items, and the money she gives away is priced nowhere

- Kind: missing-flow | Manual workaround adequate: false

**Scenario.** Bea is signed in and orders four boxes at S$68 with S$8 delivery, applies CNY10 for S$10 off and spends 240 points for another S$12 off. Checkout writes discount_cents = 2200, points_redeemed = 240, promo_code = 'CNY10', total_cents = 5400. Bea PayNows S$54. Michelle opens the order on her phone: four item lines adding up to S$68, then a single line reading 'Total (incl. delivery) S$54.00'. There is no subtotal, no delivery fee, no discount row, no promo code and no points anywhere on the screen. She has to decide from that whether Bea underpaid by S$22. Her only routes out of the panel are Bea's own tracking page, which does show 'Discount (CNY10) -S$22.00', except the admin has no link to it and no tracking token appears anywhere in the admin UI, or the New order email in her inbox, which shows the same single merged line and only if OWNER_NOTIFICATION_EMAIL was configured. At quarter end she wants to know what CNY10 actually cost her: the Promos screen shows '3 redemptions' and no money at all.

**Why a defect audit would miss it.** The discount is displayed correctly in both places anyone thought to check, so every display-side test passes: the customer's tracking page renders it and the confirmation email renders it. The admin was built around the operational question, what do I bake and has it been paid, so its order type grew the fields that answer that. rowToAdminOrder is a long, careful mapper that reads as exhaustive, and fetchAdminOrders selects `*`, so the data is on the wire and just falls on the floor. A defect audit compares each mapped field against its column and finds every one correct; it has no way to notice three columns that were never asked for. And nothing breaks, so nothing ever reports it.

**Consequence.** Individual orders with a promo or redeemed points cannot be reconciled from the panel alone, which for a shop whose normal payment is a bank transfer she eyeballs against an order means she is guessing whether she was paid in full. Worse at the aggregate level: the cost of the loyalty programme and the cost of every promo are recorded once, as one opaque number, on a screen she never sees, and are reported nowhere in dollars ever. The two halves cannot even be separated after the fact, because discount_cents is stored as promoDiscount + pointsDiscount and reconstructing the points half needs point_value_cents, which she can change in Settings at any time. Insights compounds it: per-product revenue is unit price times quantity at full list price with no allocation of the order-level discount, so Top treats and the estimated profit figure are gross of every promo, every point redeemed and every refund, sitting on the same page as a range revenue figure that is net of all three. Two money figures on one screen that can never be made to agree.

**Suggested resolution.** Carry discount_cents, promo_code and points_redeemed into OrderRow and AdminOrder and render the same four-line breakdown the customer already sees, subtotal, delivery, discount with its code, total, in the admin order modal. That is a mapper change and a few lines of JSX and it closes the per-order question completely. Then split the stored discount into promo_discount_cents and points_discount_cents at insert so the two costs are separable forever, and add a discounts-given figure to Insights and a money column beside the redemption count on the Promos screen. Allocating the order-level discount pro rata across lines before computing per-product revenue would make Top treats and range revenue agree, which is the smaller but more embarrassing half.

**Evidence.** src/lib/order.ts:212-231 AdminOrder has no discountCents, promoCode or pointsRedeemed; src/lib/admin-db.ts:39-64 OrderRow omits the three columns and :68-102 rowToAdminOrder never maps them, although :119 selects `*`. src/app/admin/(panel)/orders/page.tsx:701-705 is the only money line in the modal. src/app/track/[token]/page.tsx:377-380 and src/lib/email.ts:129-133 do show it. src/app/checkout/actions.ts:667 `{ pointsRedeemed, discountCents: promoDiscount + pointsDiscount }` is where the two merge. src/app/admin/(panel)/settings/page.tsx:106 into src/lib/admin-db.ts:1323 makes point_value_cents editable after the fact. src/lib/admin-db.ts:1430-1441 tallies promo redemptions as a count only; src/lib/admin-actions.ts:396 and :462 are create and delete with no update. src/app/admin/(panel)/analytics/page.tsx:197 `e.revenueCents += item.unitPriceCents * item.quantity`, with :221 and :239-241 building profit from it.

### 4. [MEDIUM] A manual order Michelle mistyped cannot be corrected once it is marked paid, and the only route out lies to the customer and re-dates the money

- Kind: unreachable-or-stuck-state | Manual workaround adequate: false

**Scenario.** A WhatsApp customer PayNows S$58 for two banana loaves and a box of cookies. Michelle opens New order, types it in from the chat, and fat-fingers a quantity so the order saves as S$85. She taps Mark paid, and paid_at is stamped today. Now nothing in the panel can fix it. Remove lines refuses outright because the order is paid. There is no add-items and no edit-line action in the admin at all: her complete set of order operations is status, payment, note, reschedule, deposit, cancel, record refund, remove items and new order. Record a refund can only ever reduce a total and caps at total_cents, so if the typo overstated the order the only way to make the books agree is to record a S$27 refund that never happened, which puts a fictional outflow into order_refunds. If the typo understated it, she typed S$58 for an order the customer paid S$85 for, there is no way to raise a total from the admin at all. The only honest correction is Cancel order and retype, which emails the customer that their order was cancelled and that 'if you had already paid, we'll be in touch about the refund', and tells Michelle to send S$85 back, which she must ignore, and gives the retyped order today's paid_at rather than the day the money arrived.

**Why a defect audit would miss it.** The paid-order lock is a deliberate, correct rule and it was reasoned about carefully: removeOrderItems refuses on a paid order with an explicit comment saying total_cents is the only record of what the customer actually handed over, and that is right. The rule assumes total_cents is a fact about a payment that already happened. On a manual order that assumption is false, because total_cents is whatever Michelle's thumb typed thirty seconds ago and the payment is a separate real event she is copying from WhatsApp. createManualOrder and the paid-order lock were designed independently and each is sound on its own; the gap only exists where they meet. Every prior audit that touched this ground looked at the case where reality changed after the order was correct (a short bake, a substitution), which is a genuinely different question, and closed it.

**Consequence.** The one order type where the totals are hand-entered, and therefore the one most likely to be wrong, is the one type with no correction path. Every workaround corrupts something: a fictional refund row poisons the refunds record that the fix for the first finding would depend on, and a cancel-and-retype sends a false cancellation email to a customer whose cake is fine, plus tells Michelle to refund money she is keeping, plus moves the payment into whatever month she noticed the typo. In a shop where she takes orders verbally over WhatsApp and types them in herself, this is not a rare path.

**Suggested resolution.** Do not reopen editing paid orders in general, that rule is settled and it is a good one. Add one narrow correction instead: on an order with no Stripe payment intent, let her set the charged amount to the figure that actually landed in her bank, and write the change as its own dated adjustment row rather than by mutating total_cents in place, so the correction has an audit trail and the original typo stays visible. If that is more than the launch warrants, the cheap version is to suppress the cancellation email when the cancel is immediately followed by a manual re-entry, or simply add a confirm on Cancel order that offers 'this was a typo, do not email the customer', and let the retyped order accept a backdated paid_at.

**Evidence.** src/lib/admin-db.ts:854-858 removeOrderItems refuses a paid or refunded order; :844 is the whole function and it is the only mutation of order_items available to the admin. src/lib/admin-actions.ts:179-295 is the complete list of order operations Michelle has; there is no add-items or edit-line action, and add_items_to_order is reachable only from the customer's tracking page (src/app/track/actions.ts:386). src/lib/admin-db.ts:762-770 recordRefund caps at total_cents and only ever reduces. src/lib/orders-db.ts:292-370 createManualOrder sends no emails; src/lib/admin-db.ts:711-720 the cancel path does send sendOrderCancelledEmail (src/lib/email.ts:315), and admin-db.ts:672 returns manualRefundDue on a paid PayNow order so the panel tells her to send the full amount back (orders/page.tsx:486-492). src/lib/admin-db.ts:958 stamps paid_at with new Date() and nothing accepts a backdate.

### 5. [MEDIUM] Once Michelle confirms an order, nothing in the system can ever close it if the customer then stops replying

- Kind: missing-flow | Manual workaround adequate: false

**Scenario.** Mon 3 Aug: Priya orders 2 dozen brownies and a banana loaf for Sat 8 Aug, S$98, using promo WELCOME10 and 200 of her loyalty points. She messages on WhatsApp, Michelle replies with the PayNow details and moves the order to Confirmed. Priya then goes quiet and never transfers. Fri 7 and Sat 8 the dashboard warns "1 order is due to bake today or tomorrow but still unpaid". On Sun 9 Aug that warning vanishes, because unpaidDueSoon matches only scheduledDate === today || tomorrow. From that moment there is no path out of the state. The hourly sweep can never reach the row: it requires status 'received' and Michelle's confirm moved it to 'confirmed', and it separately requires updated_at === created_at, which that same confirm already broke. The orders list shows no marker, because bakeDateHasPassed requires payment_status 'paid'. So the order lives forever: the bake list has no date floor and sorts soonest first, so "Sat 8 Aug, 1 order, 24x Brownie, 1x Banana loaf" sits above today's work every morning; Insights' "To collect" keeps counting S$98 that will never arrive; the dashboard's "Awaiting payment" keeps counting it; Priya's 200 points stay subtracted from her spendable balance on every future checkout; and WELCOME10 keeps one redemption spent against its cap. Nothing ever names the order or asks Michelle to decide.

**Why a defect audit would miss it.** The sweep was deliberately tightened after the previous audit found it was a live-order shredder, and its own comment states the intent honestly: "Anything else means a human was involved, and a human should decide." That is a correct decision, but no surface was ever built where the human is asked to decide. Every guard reads as complete on its own: the seven conditions are all defensible, the dashboard banner is deliberately scoped to the two days when chasing still helps, and the paid-late banner is deliberately paid-only because it is about rescheduling a bake. A defect audit checks each predicate against its stated purpose and each one passes. What is missing is the mirror image of the paid-late flow that was just built: there is a surface for "the money came late" and none for "the money never came, and I had already accepted the order". Confirming an order is the very first thing Michelle does on WhatsApp, so the normal path is precisely the one the sweep can no longer see.

**Consequence.** The two money figures Michelle would use to decide who to chase, "To collect" in Insights and "Awaiting payment" on the dashboard, drift permanently upward with dead orders and can only ever grow. A ghosting customer's redeemed loyalty points are held against them forever on every future order, and a capped promo code silently loses a redemption slot for good. The bake list, the sheet she actually works from, permanently opens with a day that will never be baked. The only resolution is that Michelle remembers, months later, and cancels by hand, which then emails the customer "your order has been cancelled" long after everyone has moved on.

**Suggested resolution.** Give the state a surface rather than widening the sweep, since the owner deliberately narrowed it. Add an "overdue and unpaid" banner to the orders list that is the exact sibling of bakeDateHasPassed: payment_status is not paid, status is received or confirmed, and scheduled_date < today, sorted to the top the same way, with two buttons, Chase payment (the WhatsApp link that already exists) and Cancel. Give the bake list a floor so past days are collapsed behind a "3 past days still open" line rather than sitting above today's bake. If nothing else is done, at minimum bound the points-held and promo-redemption counts to orders whose scheduled_date has not passed, so a ghosted order stops penalising the customer and the code.

**Evidence.** src/lib/order-cleanup.ts:54 .eq("status", "received") and :72 `if (Date.parse(row.updated_at) !== Date.parse(row.created_at)) continue;` are the two conditions Michelle's own confirm breaks (status set at src/lib/admin-db.ts:133-136, which writes both status and updated_at). src/app/admin/(panel)/page.tsx:90-95 unpaidDueSoon matches only `o.scheduledDate === today || o.scheduledDate === tomorrow`. src/app/admin/(panel)/orders/page.tsx:83-89 bakeDateHasPassed requires `order.paymentStatus === "paid"`, and :181-196 shows the only list filters are the seven statuses, with the sort at :130-138 falling back to created_at. src/app/admin/(panel)/bake-list/page.tsx:19-21 filters only on status with no date floor and :50 sorts ascending. src/app/admin/(panel)/analytics/page.tsx:72-80 toCollectCents counts every pending non-cancelled order with no date bound; src/app/admin/(panel)/page.tsx:64-66 does the same for the Awaiting payment tile. src/app/checkout/actions.ts:609-619 subtracts points_redeemed on every pending or failed non-cancelled order from the customer's spendable balance, unbounded by date. src/lib/promos.ts:93-100 counts the global redemption cap over non-cancelled orders regardless of payment.

### 6. [MEDIUM] Michelle can only subtract from an order, and only a whole line at a time, so the everyday WhatsApp change "make it two instead of three" cannot be recorded at all

- Kind: missing-flow | Manual workaround adequate: false

**Scenario.** Nadia orders 3 boxes of brownies at S$30, one banana loaf at S$18 and S$8 delivery for Sunday, signed in, 100 points redeemed, total S$111, unpaid. Friday morning, inside the 2-day lead time, she WhatsApps: "sorry, make it 2 boxes not 3". Michelle says yes. In the admin the only tool is Remove items, and it removes whole lines: the checkbox list has no quantity control and remove_items_from_order takes only an array of item ids, so the single expressible move is to take the entire 3-box line off and drop S$90 from an order that should only fall by S$30. Nothing can put 2 boxes back, because add_items_to_order has exactly one caller in the codebase and it is the customer's own tracking panel, which by Friday is closed on two counts: it refuses once scheduled_date is inside the lead time and it refuses once the order is paid. So the app can say "3 boxes" or "no brownies" and nothing in between. The same asymmetry runs the other way: when Nadia instead asks on Friday to add one more loaf, Michelle agrees, bakes it, and has no way to put it on the order or charge for it. If the order had been a single line, a common shape here ("1x 8-inch Basque cheesecake"), even subtraction is refused, because the panel blocks removing every line and tells her to cancel instead. What she actually does is leave the order at S$111, bake 2 boxes, and when Nadia's S$81 PayNow lands, tap Mark paid. paid_at is stamped correctly and S$111 is booked as revenue for S$81 received; the packing slip prints 3 boxes and the shopping list buys ingredients for 3.

**Why a defect audit would miss it.** The previous audit found that orders could only grow, and the fix shipped is genuinely correct code: remove_items_from_order moves the rows and both totals in one transaction, and every guard on it is defensible. But the fix implemented line deletion, not the "remove a line or change its quantity" that the finding actually asked for, and it added no counterpart on the admin side of add_items_to_order. Nobody re-checked the shape of the residue, because the operation that is missing has no code to inspect. The asymmetry only becomes visible when you line the two windows up: subtraction is open right up to the oven (removeOrderItems has no lead-time check at all), while addition closes two days before the date, closes the moment payment lands, is gated behind a feature flag, and can only be performed by the customer on their own phone.

**Consequence.** The order row stops describing what was agreed at exactly the point where the new paid_at machinery is meant to make the books reconcile: Mark paid stamps the right date against the wrong amount, and there is no field anywhere that can express "the customer paid less than this order says" or "the customer paid more". The production surfaces stay wrong too, so the bake sheet and the shopping list are for food nobody is getting. The one truthful alternative, cancel and retype it as a manual order, is destructive: it emails the customer that their order was cancelled, mints a new order number and tracking link they are never sent because manual orders deliberately send no customer email, writes user_id null so a signed-in customer earns nothing and the order never appears in her account, and silently drops the 100 points she redeemed along with any promo discount.

**Suggested resolution.** Extend the existing pair rather than adding a new concept.
