# Michelle's Munchies - logic gap audit

Date: 2026-07-30. Branch: brand-refresh. This audit deliberately hunted a DIFFERENT bug class from the two before it.

The previous audits found code defects: races, injection, missing guards. All 58 are fixed. This one hunted LOGIC GAPS IN THE MODEL - places where every line of code is correct but the design has a hole, so reality can put the system in a state nobody built a path for.

The calibration example was the owner's own finding: scheduled_date is the date the customer picked and nothing moves it when payment arrives late, so a late-paid order sits permanently in the past. No defect to point at, simply no flow.

Method: 8 reasoning lenses (temporal coherence, state completeness, out-of-order events, semantic drift, missing flows, settings under in-flight orders, money reconciliation, conflicting human intent), plus a pass for the unexamined dimension. Every gap was then attacked by a skeptic whose main test was: for a one-person bakery on WhatsApp, does messaging the customer resolve this completely with no wrong data left? If yes it was refuted.

## Totals

- Raised: 53
- Refuted by the challenge stage: 36
- CONFIRMED: 17 findings, covering 13 distinct root causes

## 1. [MEDIUM] Stock is committed at order time but only reserved at payment time, so unlimited unpaid PayNow orders can oversell the same units

- Kind: temporal-drift | Lens: temporal | Manual workaround adequate: false

**Scenario.** Michelle sets stock_count = 12 on Basque Cheesecake because 12 is all she can fit in the oven for Saturday. Monday 9am: Aisha orders 12 for Saturday, gets the order number, and messages Michelle on WhatsApp to arrange PayNow. The order is written with payment_status 'pending'. Nothing decrements stock_count, so it is still 12 and is_available is still true. Monday 11am: Bala loads the same product page, sees no sold-out badge, and orders 12 for Saturday. Monday 2pm: Chloe orders 12. Michelle now has three confirmed-looking orders for 36 cheesecakes on a day she declared she could make 12. Wednesday she reconciles her bank and marks all three paid one after another. The first paid order takes stock 12 to 0 and auto-sells-out the product. The second and third run adjust_product_stock with delta -12 against a count of 0, which greatest(0, v_old + p_delta) floors back to 0. No error, no warning, no trace: stock_count reads 0 as if 12 were sold, three orders exist for 36, and the bake list for Saturday says 36. The same hole applies to the manual-order path, which never looks at stock_count at all.

**Why a defect audit missed it.** Every individual line of this is correct. resolveCartLines really does refuse a cart line bigger than the live stock_count. adjust_product_stock really is atomic under SELECT ... FOR UPDATE and really does prevent two concurrent orders clobbering each other's write. decrementStockForOrder really is claimed exactly once per order. A defect audit checks each guard against its own stated purpose and each one passes. The gap is that the guard was designed for a world where placing an order and paying for it are the same instant (Stripe), and it was carried into a business where they are days apart. The author's own comment at cart-resolve.ts:124 gives it away: it says "one cart can't commit Michelle to more than she has" - one cart is exactly the scope, and the bakery's problem is many carts over many days.

**Consequence.** Michelle is committed to baking more of a limited item than she declared she could make, having taken PayNow money for all of it. She either bakes through the night, or tells two paying customers their order is not happening, and she has to refund a bank transfer by hand. stock_count is also permanently wrong afterwards (it reads 0 having 'sold' 36 from 12), which corrupts the low-stock alert threshold and the auto-sold-out/back-in-stock machinery for that product from then on. It is the only per-item cap the system has - daily_order_cap and per_window_cap count orders, not units - so nothing else catches it.

**Suggested resolution.** Reserve stock when the order row is written, not when payment lands, and release it when the order is cancelled. Concretely: move the adjust_product_stock decrement inside create_order_with_items alongside the existing capacity checks (it already holds an advisory lock), and have it raise a marker like 'stock_short:<name>' that placeOrder translates the way it already translates capacity_day_full. That makes reservation and commitment the same instant again. If reserving at order time is unacceptable because unpaid orders would then hold stock, the cheaper alternative is to keep decrementing at payment but check the requested quantity against stock_count minus the quantity already on open non-cancelled unpaid orders for that product, so the count that gates a new order is the uncommitted count rather than the unpaid-and-still-full count.

**Evidence.** src/lib/cart-resolve.ts:127 is the only stock gate on the checkout path and compares against the live, undecremented product.stockCount (its own comment at :124-126 scopes itself to "one cart"). Stock is decremented only from src/lib/admin-db.ts:517 decrementStockForOrder, called only at src/lib/admin-db.ts:603 inside markOrderPaid, which is reached from the Stripe webhook and from the by-hand path at src/lib/admin-db.ts:238-239. supabase/migrations/0000_baseline_schema.sql:886 floors the result with greatest(0, v_old + p_delta), so the overshoot is silently absorbed. supabase/migrations/0000_baseline_schema.sql:748-769 shows create_order_with_items checks only the daily and window caps under its lock and never touches stock. src/app/checkout/actions.ts:496-519 confirms the contrast: the order caps deliberately count non-cancelled orders regardless of payment, so the two capacity mechanisms in the system run on two different clocks. src/lib/orders-db.ts:222-296 createManualOrder has no stock check of any kind. src/components/product/ProductCard.tsx:32 drives the Sold out badge purely from is_available, which only flips at the paid transition.

**Challenge note (this corrects or sharpens the claim above).** Keep the finding, drop it from high to medium, and fix two things in how it is stated.

1. Severity: medium, not high. Live DB (queried now, ddwesutmtlytbcluqcuc) has 23 products and zero with stock_count set, so nothing is tracked and the hole is armed only when Michelle types a number into the Stock field. No customer-facing count exists either (ProductCard.tsx:32 keys off isAvailable only; there is no "N left" copy anywhere), so no customer is misled before a payment lands, and the bake list at src/app/admin/(panel)/bake-list/page.tsx:20-45 does aggregate unpaid orders, so she has a daily surface where "36x" would appear. docs/site-audit-2026-07-29.md:122 rated the single-cart half of this same mechanism medium for the same latency reason.

2. Add the strongest consequence, which the claim missed. The obvious repair makes the data worse. Cancelling or un-marking-paid the extra order runs reversePaidSideEffects (src/lib/admin-db.ts:355-390), which calls adjust_product_stock with +12 on a row already floored to 0 with auto_disabled true. Per 0000_baseline_schema.sql:886-908 that yields v_new = 12 and re_enabled = true, so is_available flips back to true and src/lib/admin-db.ts:385-388 emails the back-in-stock waitlist about 12 units of a product that is still 24 units oversold. This is what defeats the "a WhatsApp message fixes it" objection and should be the headline consequence rather than "stock_count reads 0".

3. Correct two overstatements. First, the scenario's framing of stock_count as "12 is all she can fit in the oven for Saturday" misreads the column: it is a single global count with no date dimension (schema :169), so it can never express per-day oven capacity even if it were reserved at order time. State it as a limited item or one-off drop of 12 units. Second, drop the manual-order half. createManualOrder skipping stock matches its documented intent at src/lib/orders-db.ts:254-255, "Michelle is logging an order she already accepted, so she decides, not the caps", which also waives the capacity caps. That is deliberate owner override, not a gap.

## 2. [MEDIUM] A customer-initiated reschedule moves the bake date and tells nobody, including on an order Michelle has already shopped for and is baking

- Kind: missing-flow | Lens: event-order | Manual workaround adequate: false

**Scenario.** Wei places a $85 order (two cakes) on Monday for Saturday, PayNow. Michelle confirms it on WhatsApp; status 'confirmed', payment_status 'pending' because Wei pays on collection day. Friday morning Michelle opens the shopping list, which is derived live from scheduled_date (src/app/admin/(panel)/shopping-list/page.tsx:62-79), buys cream and fresh strawberries for Saturday, and prints Saturday's bake sheet. Friday 11pm Wei opens the tracking link from his confirmation email and moves the order to the following Wednesday. rescheduleOrderAction accepts it: the order is unpaid so isChangeable(status) is still true (src/app/track/actions.ts:59), Wednesday clears the lead time and both caps, and the UPDATE writes scheduled_date, time_window and reschedule_count and returns (src/app/track/actions.ts:106-116). No email is sent to anyone. No line is written on owner_note. Saturday morning the order has silently vanished from the bake list and the dashboard's "Bake for today", and Michelle's ticked-off progress from Friday is orphaned because the tick keys are `${date}::${label}` (src/app/admin/(panel)/bake-list/page.tsx:121,163). She either bakes from the printed sheet for a customer who is not coming, or discovers it only if she re-opens the panel. Because payment is never checked here, the same move works on an order she has already been paid for and is physically baking, as long as she has not yet flipped the status to 'baking'.

**Why a defect audit missed it.** The function is a model of careful validation: rate limit, feature flag, changeable-status gate, reschedule cap, lead time, cutoff, blackout, and both capacity caps recounted excluding the order's own row. An auditor checking for missing guards finds none and stops. The gap is the absence of an outbound notification, which is not a guard at all. It only becomes visible by comparing the three sibling self-serve actions in the same file and next door, every one of which does notify: addItemsToOrderAction sends sendItemsAddedEmail, requestCancellationAction writes a marker into owner_note and emails, scheduleGiftAction emails. The admin reschedule even emails the customer. Reschedule is the one hole in an otherwise complete pattern.

**Consequence.** Perishable ingredients bought for a bake that moved, and potentially a finished bake nobody collects, on the one action a customer can take up to the night before. The reschedule_count the system carefully increments is stored (orders.reschedule_count, baseline schema line 319) and read only as a cap; it is never mapped into AdminOrder (src/lib/admin-db.ts:74-97) so Michelle cannot even see that an order has been moved, let alone that it was moved by the customer. A capacity slot also silently migrates between days.

**Suggested resolution.** Mirror what the neighbouring actions already do. After the successful UPDATE in rescheduleOrderAction, send the owner a notification naming the order, the old date and the new one (an owner-addressed sibling of sendRescheduleEmail, using OWNER_NOTIFICATION_EMAIL like sendItemsAddedEmail does), and append a dated line to owner_note the way requestCancellationAction does so the record survives an unread inbox. Surface reschedule_count in AdminOrder and show "moved by customer" on the order row. Consider refusing a self-serve move inside the lead-time window on an order that is already paid, and pointing the customer at WhatsApp instead.

**Evidence.** src/app/track/actions.ts:38-117 is the whole action; the UPDATE at :106-114 is followed directly by `return { ok: true }` at :116 with no send call. Contrast src/lib/admin-db.ts:296-313 (admin reschedule does call sendRescheduleEmail), src/app/track/actions.ts:289 (sendItemsAddedEmail to the owner), :329-344 (owner_note line plus sendCancellationRequestEmail), src/app/gift/actions.ts:101 (sendGiftScheduledEmail to the owner). No payment check exists in the action. src/lib/email.ts has no owner-facing reschedule function (the only reschedule sender, :323-338, addresses params.email, the customer). Bake list and shopping list both key off scheduledDate: src/app/admin/(panel)/bake-list/page.tsx:18-59, src/app/admin/(panel)/shopping-list/page.tsx:62-79.

**Challenge note (this corrects or sharpens the claim above).** Claim stands at medium, but narrow it to the sharp half and drop the padding. The sharp half is the missing temporal gate, not the missing email: rescheduleOrderAction validates only the NEW date against earliestFulfillmentDate (src/app/track/actions.ts:67-104) and never asks how imminent the CURRENT scheduled_date is, so an order Michelle has already shopped and possibly baked for can be moved away at 11pm the night before. A move made two weeks out is harmless on its own and would be low. The decisive evidence is the sibling asymmetry in the same file: addItemsToOrderAction refuses paid orders (:184), refuses when order.scheduled_date < earliest with copy routing the customer to WhatsApp (:190-200), is hidden at page level on payment_status !== "paid" (src/app/track/[token]/page.tsx:86-89), and emails the owner (:289). Reschedule has none of those four. The codebase has already ruled that a self-serve change inside the lead time must be refused; reschedule does not follow the rule. Verified with real lines: the UPDATE at :106-114 is followed directly by return { ok: true } at :116; src/lib/email.ts has no owner-facing reschedule sender (the only one, :323-338, is documented as the customer notification for an ADMIN move and sends to params.email); src/app/api/cron/route.ts runs no bake digest; reschedule_count and updated_at are both absent from OrderRow and rowToAdminOrder (src/lib/admin-db.ts:37-98) so no admin screen can show that an order was moved; bake-list/page.tsx:18-59 and shopping-list/page.tsx:62-79 both derive live from scheduledDate; feature_order_changes defaults true (0000_baseline_schema.sql:538) and the panel renders on features.orderChanges && isChangeable(status) alone (track/[token]/page.tsx:348). Not a duplicate: audit finding #11 covered add-items only and its fix touched only addItemsToOrderAction. Overstated parts to cut: no data is corrupted and money reconciles fine, so this is weaker than the paid-late archetype; the capacity slot moving between days is correct behaviour, not harm; the orphaned bake ticks are weak, and the real tick defect is that ${date}::${label} aggregates across orders so a same-day duplicate item stays ticked at a reduced quantity, which is a pre-existing weakness of the tick model rather than of this action. What keeps it at medium rather than low is that this is the one write a customer can make to Michelle's production schedule, it leaves no trace anyone can see, and both parties are actively misled: the customer is told "Your order has been moved. Thank you!" under copy reading "before we start baking" while that phrase is gated on a status flag she may not have flipped mid-bake. Minimal fix: refuse when scheduled_date < earliest and route to WhatsApp exactly as add-items now does, send an owner email on the moves that are still allowed, and map reschedule_count into AdminOrder so a moved order is visible.

## 3. [MEDIUM] stock_count only moves when payment is marked, so it means "units not yet paid for" while every guard reads it as "units still available to sell"

- Kind: semantic-drift | Lens: semantics | Manual workaround adequate: false

**Scenario.** Michelle lists a limited seasonal item, Yuzu Tart, and types 3 into the Stock field. Monday morning customer A orders 3 tarts for Saturday. The order is written by create_order_with_items (supabase/migrations/0000_baseline_schema.sql:737-823), which touches only `orders` and `order_items` and takes nothing from stock. products.stock_count is still 3. Monday afternoon customer B orders 3 for Saturday: resolveCartLines checks `product.stockCount != null && line.quantity > product.stockCount` (src/lib/cart-resolve.ts:127), reads 3, and accepts. Tuesday customer C does the same. Three confirmation emails go out promising 9 tarts. All three pay by PayNow over the next two days. When Michelle reconciles her bank on Thursday and taps Mark paid three times, updatePaymentStatus (src/lib/admin-db.ts:239) calls markOrderPaid, which calls decrementStockForOrder (src/lib/admin-db.ts:517), which calls adjust_product_stock. That function clamps with `greatest(0, v_old + p_delta)` (baseline:886), so 3 becomes 0 on the first order and stays 0 for the other two. The 6-unit overcommitment leaves no trace anywhere. The low-stock email (src/lib/admin-db.ts:548-555) fires on the same Thursday, days after she was already oversold. Michelle finds out reading the bake list on Friday night.

**Why a defect audit missed it.** The previous audit found exactly the right symptom ("Checkout never checks stock_count") and the fix added the check at src/lib/cart-resolve.ts:127 and src/app/track/actions.ts:230. The check is correct code. What nobody asked is what the number it reads actually means: stock_count is written in only two places, the admin product editor and adjust_product_stock, and adjust_product_stock is only ever reached from the paid transition. In a Stripe-first shop payment is synchronous and the counter is close enough to "available". In this shop the normal path is WhatsApp plus PayNow marked by hand days later, so the counter lags the sold quantity by the entire unpaid backlog, which is most of the order book. There is no defect to point at, only a missing concept: nothing reserves stock at order time.

**Consequence.** A limited item can be sold an unbounded number of times. Michelle either bakes at a loss to honour the promises or tells paying customers she cannot make what they already paid for. The auto-sell-out and the low-stock email, both of which hang off the same counter, fire days late for the same reason, so the two safety nets that were supposed to catch this also only work on a Stripe timeline.

**Suggested resolution.** Introduce a reserved quantity that moves at order time, not payment time, and read that at the sell gates. Simplest form: keep stock_count as physical stock, and compute committed units as the sum of order_items.quantity across non-cancelled orders for the product, then gate on `stock_count - committed`. Best done inside create_order_with_items, which already holds an advisory lock on the scheduled date, so the check and the insert are atomic like the capacity caps already are. Keep the paid-time decrement as the physical adjustment. Rename the field in the admin editor to "units I can make" so it reads as a ceiling rather than a live count.

**Evidence.** src/lib/cart-resolve.ts:127 (only sell-side read of stockCount); src/app/track/actions.ts:230 (same, top-up flow); src/lib/admin-db.ts:517-557 decrementStockForOrder, reached only from src/lib/admin-db.ts:603 inside markOrderPaid, itself called from src/lib/admin-db.ts:239 (admin Mark paid) and src/app/api/stripe/webhook/route.ts:90; supabase/migrations/0000_baseline_schema.sql:866-910 adjust_product_stock with the greatest(0,...) clamp at :886; supabase/migrations/0000_baseline_schema.sql:737-823 create_order_with_items, which locks only on capacity and never reads stock_count; src/lib/admin-db.ts:710 is the only other writer (product editor). No reservation table or column exists in the baseline. Live: 23 products, 0 with stock_count set, so this is latent until she uses the field.

**Challenge note (this corrects or sharpens the claim above).** Real and worth acting on, but medium rather than high, and the write-up needs three corrections.

DOWNGRADE TO MEDIUM: Latent. Live DB (ddwesutmtlytbcluqcuc): 23 products, 0 with stock_count set, all 23 available. The prior audit held the sibling finding (#7, docs/site-audit-2026-07-29.md:130) at medium on exactly this ground. Nothing arms until Michelle types a number into the Stock field (src/app/admin/(panel)/products/page.tsx:470-484). It belongs at the top of medium and becomes high the day one product is tracked.

OVERSTATED - discovery timing. "Michelle finds out reading the bake list on Friday night" is wrong. The bake list (src/app/admin/(panel)/bake-list/page.tsx:20-44) aggregates quantity per product label per scheduled date across every order that is not completed or cancelled, paid or unpaid, so "9x Yuzu Tart" is on Saturday's sheet the moment the third order lands on Tuesday. It is never compared against stock_count so it does not warn her, but the number is in front of her days earlier than claimed. Also drop the "actively misled about availability" framing: the storefront never displays remaining units (no "only N left" anywhere in src/components or src/app/menu), so the customers see only an available product, not a count they can disprove.

UNDERSTATED 1 - the manual fix corrupts the counter and lies to the waitlist. This is the strongest part of the finding and it is missing. Michelle's only resolution is to cancel the two extra paid orders, which runs reversePaidSideEffects (src/lib/admin-db.ts:355-390). It calls adjust_product_stock with +quantity, and the greatest(0, v_old + p_delta) clamp at baseline:886 is not remembered on the way up: the counter goes 0 -> 3 -> 6. The +3 that crosses zero also satisfies v_re_enabled (baseline:885, v_old <= 0 and v_new > 0 and auto_disabled), so is_available flips back to true and src/lib/admin-db.ts:383-388 fires notifySubscribers, emailing the back-in-stock waitlist that a fully sold-out, unmakeable product is back with 6 units nobody will ever bake. So the WhatsApp-and-cancel workaround does not resolve the state, it manufactures a worse one, and Michelle must know to go zero the Stock field by hand. That is what carries this past the "a quick message fixes it" bar.

UNDERSTATED 2 - the precedent is already in the codebase. src/app/checkout/actions.ts:583-593 subtracts points held on the customer's placed-but-unpaid orders from their spendable balance, with the comment "Points redeemed on this customer's placed-but-unpaid orders are not debited from the ledger until the order is marked paid, so subtract them from the balance here. Otherwise the same points could be redeemed again on a second, concurrent order and drive the ledger negative." That is the exact reservation this finding says is missing, implemented for loyalty points and omitted for stock. Cite it: it turns "nobody thought about it" into a demonstrable inconsistency and points straight at the fix shape (have resolveCartLines and track/actions.ts compare quantity against stock_count minus units on non-cancelled unpaid orders, rather than moving the decrement earlier).

RELATIONSHIP TO FIXED #7 - state it explicitly so this does not read as a re-report. Fixed finding #7 is titled "oversold in a single order" and its fix is the per-line clamp now at src/lib/cart-resolve.ts:127. That clamp is structurally unable to catch this case, where every order is individually within stock. Same counter and same Yuzu Tart example, different mechanism, and the residual hole is unbounded where #7 was capped at 99 units and needed a customer deliberately cranking a stepper.

## 4. [MEDIUM] Nothing records when the money actually arrived, so no period's revenue can be tied to the bank statement

- Kind: temporal-drift | Lens: reconciliation | Manual workaround adequate: not stated

**Scenario.** Amy orders on 26 June for a 30 June birthday, S$120, and pays by PayNow on 3 July. Michelle sees the transfer in her bank on 3 July and taps Mark paid. The row now says payment_status = paid. The only timestamps on it are created_at (26 June, immutable) and updated_at, which the very next status change to confirmed, then baking, then completed overwrites. There is no paid_at column. On 31 July Michelle opens Insights, picks This month, and the Revenue card shows a figure built by bucketing each paid order on singaporeDateString(o.createdAt). Amy's S$120 lands in the June bucket even though the cash arrived in July, and four July orders still awaiting PayNow are absent entirely. Her bank shows S$4,200 received in July; the panel says S$3,650 for July and S$3,890 for June, and no combination of screens can be made to produce either bank total. She also cannot list which orders were paid in July, because that fact was never written down anywhere.

**Why a defect audit missed it.** Every line is correct in isolation. markOrderPaid does exactly what it says, the analytics reducer sums exactly what it reads, and dayOf() is a correct Singapore-date helper. A defect audit asks whether createdAt is bucketed correctly, and it is. Nobody asks whether createdAt is the right date to bucket by, because in a card-checkout shop it is: order date and payment date are the same minute. In this bakery they are routinely days or weeks apart, and that fact was never given a column.

**Consequence.** The bakery's books cannot be reconciled against the bank for any month, quarter or year. Revenue is systematically attributed to the wrong period for every PayNow order, which is the normal path. Because the payment date is never persisted and updated_at is clobbered by the next status change, the information needed to fix a past period is destroyed, not merely unreported.

**Suggested resolution.** Add orders.paid_at, stamp it in markOrderPaid on the guarded pending-to-paid update (and clear it in reversePaidSideEffects), then bucket every Insights revenue figure on paid_at rather than created_at, falling back to created_at only for rows written before the column existed. Keep Booked next 14 days on scheduled_date and To collect date-free, since those are forward-looking.

**Evidence.** supabase/migrations/0000_baseline_schema.sql:289-335 lists every orders column: created_at and updated_at exist, no paid_at. src/lib/admin-db.ts:564-583 markOrderPaid writes only payment_status, an optional intent id, and updated_at. src/lib/admin-db.ts:126 updateOrderStatus overwrites updated_at on every later status move. src/app/admin/(panel)/analytics/page.tsx:53 dayOf, :78 firstPaidDay, :167-171 `const day = dayOf(o.createdAt) ... rangeRevenue += o.totalCents`, :196-199 the previous-period comparison, all keyed on createdAt. A repo-wide grep for paid_at, paidAt, refunded_at returns nothing.

**Challenge note (this corrects or sharpens the claim above).** Real and not a duplicate, but three parts of the write-up overstate it, so medium rather than high.

1. "Revenue is systematically attributed to the wrong period for every PayNow order" is wrong as stated. Revenue is attributed to the ORDER date for every order. Because baking is gated on payment (src/lib/admin-db.ts:128) and there is a lead time, the order-to-payment lag is hours to a few days by business necessity, so order date and payment date fall in the same month for the large majority. The misattribution is a month-boundary effect proportional to that lag, a single-digit percentage of a month's takings, and it partly cancels between adjacent months. Lifetime revenue and the "All time" range are exact.

2. "The bakery's books cannot be reconciled against the bank" overstates the app's role. The authoritative cash record is her bank statement, which does carry the date and amount of every PayNow transfer. The panel is not an accounting system: no expenses, no invoicing, no GST, no P&L, and per-product cost yields only what the code itself calls an "honestly partial" margin (analytics/page.tsx:55-56). She is not filing tax from the Revenue card.

3. "No combination of screens can be made to produce either bank total" is literally true but reads as though she is blind to uncollected money. She is not: the "To collect" tile (analytics/page.tsx:62-66, rendered 275-283) gives the exact outstanding amount and count as of now, and the dashboard adds "Awaiting payment" plus an unpaid-due-soon list (src/app/admin/(panel)/page.tsx:63-66, 85-91).

What survives, and why it is not low:
- The unrecorded fact is unrecoverable, not merely unreported. markOrderPaid stamps nothing, and src/lib/admin-db.ts:126 clobbers updated_at on the next status move, usually within hours. With no audit table, no order export, and no owner-visible payment date, a cash-basis figure for any past month cannot be reconstructed from the database at all.
- A closed month's revenue silently changes after the fact. The paid filter is applied at read time while the bucket key is createdAt, so a July-created order paid in August retroactively raises July. "Last month" read on 1 August and again on 20 August returns different July totals with nothing on screen explaining why, and the card is labelled plainly "Revenue" with no basis disclosure.
- Neither escape hatch applies: no message and no edit fixes it, because there is no field to edit.

The fix is one nullable timestamptz stamped in markOrderPaid, bucketed on with a fallback to created_at. That it is cheap is an argument for doing it, not for calling it high.

## 5. [MEDIUM] The customer's self-serve reschedule stays open right through the bake and the handover, and tells nobody it happened

- Kind: conflicting-intent | Lens: multi-actor | Manual workaround adequate: false

**Scenario.** Order MM-260808-XXXX, 3 dozen cookies, delivery, scheduled_date = Sat 8 Aug, PayNow. Michelle WhatsApps the PayNow details on the 6th. She cannot mark it paid until the transfer shows in her bank, and the payment gate means she cannot move it to `baking` either, so on bake morning the row is still `confirmed`, payment `pending`. (1) Saturday 7am she bakes it off the bake list. 9am the customer taps "Save new date" on the tracking link and moves it to Mon 10 Aug. rescheduleOrderAction validates only the NEW date, writes scheduled_date and time_window, and returns. No email to Michelle, no email to the customer, no owner alert, nothing on the dashboard. Her phone still shows Saturday's bake list because AdminStore only re-pulls on mount or on tab refocus (throttled 30s) and never polls while the tab stays in the foreground. When it eventually refreshes, the order and its items simply are not on Saturday any more, with no trace they ever were. She has baked a box nobody is coming for. (2) The nastier variant: she delivers on Saturday afternoon. The order STILL sits at `confirmed` because completed also requires paid. Sunday the customer, thinking they are booking their next batch, opens the same bookmarked link and moves the date to Tue 11 Aug. The delivered order reappears on Tuesday's bake list as live work and she bakes and delivers it a second time, unpaid.

**Why a defect audit missed it.** Every line is correct and defensible on its own. isChangeable() is a single source of truth, the reschedule re-validates lead time, cutoff, blackout and both caps, and the panel copy says "before we start baking". The hole is that `baking` is a status the paid-before-baking gate makes unreachable for the store's dominant PayNow flow, so the boundary that was supposed to close the customer's change window never closes at all. A defect audit reads each guard, finds it present and correct, and never asks whether the state it keys on can be reached. The missing owner notification is invisible for the same reason: the two sibling actions in the same file both alert Michelle, so nothing looks omitted until you diff them.

**Consequence.** Ingredients and a whole bake wasted, or in variant (2) a second batch baked and handed over for free.
